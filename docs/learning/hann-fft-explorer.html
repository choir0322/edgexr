3 / 8 · prepare and weight

Subtract the mean, then soften the patch boundary.

patch_shift() / np.hanning()

One weight per pixel. Edge 0 → center near 1.
weighted = (patch − mean) × Hann
The same weight map is applied to A and B.

For centered and weighted images, middle gray means zero; dark and bright show negative and positive values. Image display scales are illustrative.

Checks before the FFT

Require matching 2D shapes, at least 16 pixels per dimension. Convert pixels to floating point and reject nonfinite values. Reject either patch when intensity standard deviation is below 3: a flat patch has too little pattern to locate reliably.

FFT represents a finite patch using periodic patterns. A sharp join between opposite edges spreads artificial boundary energy across frequencies. Hann reduces that boundary effect; it does not identify objects or remove every source of noise.

4 / 8 · Fourier comparison

Each weighted image becomes many spatial pattern coefficients

np.fft.fft2() / conjugate

These maps come from the two recorded, fully Hann-weighted patches. Their display centers zero frequency; the estimator's arrays remain in NumPy's original order.

C = FFT(B) × conjugate(FFT(A))
R = C / max(|C|, 10⁻⁹)

Magnitude says how strongly a spatial pattern appears. Phase describes its alignment. Multiplication with the conjugate compares phase alignment. Dividing by magnitude reduces strength differences while the small floor avoids division by zero. Inverse FFT turns the normalized comparison into candidate-shift scores.

Positive dx means image content moved right; positive dy means down. The correlation grid's coordinates describe proposed motion, not where the bright object sits on the screen.

Q measures peak prominence relative to background variation, not probability. A patch also fails if |dx| > 20 or |dy| > 14 analysis pixels.
