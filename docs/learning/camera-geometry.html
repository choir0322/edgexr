Camera geometry · foundation for step 1

From a pixel to a direction

The detector reports “bottle at pixel (900, 300)”. To remember where the bottle is after the head turns away, the program needs a direction, not a pixel. The camera calibration provides the conversion.

Labels: measured recorded on the Pi · derived calculated from measured or configured numbers, not timed · illustrative teaching drawing · planned not built yet.

1 · One triangle

A pixel’s distance from the centre is an angle derived

Picture the lens as a single point. The sensor sits behind it at a distance called the focal length, measured in pixels: about 733.6 px for this camera. A pixel d pixels to the right of the image centre sits on a ray that leaves the lens at angle atan(d / 733.6). Move the slider to choose a pixel column.

Angle per pixel is not constant. Near the centre one pixel spans about 0.078°; near the edges it spans less, because the same pixel width covers a smaller angle further out. The lens adds a second, smaller effect: distortion (section 3).

2 · The camera’s angle grid

Every pixel has a direction derived

The grid below shows lines of equal direction: vertical curves are 10° steps of yaw (left/right), horizontal curves are 10° steps of pitch (up/down). They curve slightly because the lens bends light. Hover or click anywhere to read that pixel’s direction; arrow keys move the marker when the image is focused.

3 · Lens distortion

How much the lens moves a point derived

A real lens does not follow the one-triangle model exactly. OpenCV describes the error with five numbers fitted during calibration (k1, k2, k3 bend points toward or away from the centre; p1, p2 account for a slightly tilted lens). The arrows show their effect across the image, magnified; the chart shows the true size along the middle row.

4 · Where the numbers came from

Calibration from a checkerboard on a screen measured

The camera recorded the on-screen checkerboard for 300 s while the screen was moved around the view. 150 frames were sampled (every 2 s); OpenCV found all 54 inner corners in 138. It fitted the lens on 111 and held out 27 to test the fit. Reprojection error is how far the fitted model puts each corner from where it was actually seen.

Is the focal length stable?

Fitting the first and second halves of a 50-frame sample separately gave slightly different focal lengths. The spread is a rough guide to how uncertain directions are.

Status is needs_review: both halves came from the same video and screen, nearby samples are correlated, some frames had clipping and reflections, and no independent real-world check exists.

5 · Step 1 preview

Same bottle, different head angles planned illustrative

Step 1 of the spatial-awareness plan adds the head’s direction (from the gyro) to the pixel’s direction (from calibration). The bottle’s bearing, its direction from the fixed base, should then stay the same while its pixel position changes. Pan the head and watch both.

Safe commanded range for this rig: ±40°.

Simplifications: tilt held at 0°, so bearing = head pan + image yaw exactly; the lens sits a few centimetres off the pan axis, which shifts very near objects slightly and is ignored here; head pan would come from the gyro estimate, which drifts (see Head orientation).
