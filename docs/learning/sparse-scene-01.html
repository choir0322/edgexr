Why a pan/tilt head cannot do this illustrative

Depth comes from parallax: when the camera moves, near things slide across the image more than far things. For these scenes the whole rig was carried sideways by hand. EdgeXR’s head only turns. When a camera turns about its own centre, near and far things slide by the same angle, so the image changes but carries no depth. Below, a near box sits exactly in front of a far box. Compare the two motions:

Pinhole model with this camera’s 733.6 px focal length; distances are arbitrary units. The real lens sits a few centimetres off the pan axis, so panning moves it slightly and very near objects show a little parallax, too small and uncalibrated to measure depth. That is why the spatial-awareness plan gets near/far from a depth network instead (step 4).

What is measured, reconstructed, or illustrative?

The photos, camera poses and point clouds are from your Pi outputs at video times 5 and 15 seconds. Green crosses show the original cloud projected through its original calibration and pose. ORB keypoints and descriptor matches were regenerated on the Mac from those exact snapshots. They are thinned for readability.

The Pi export did not save observation IDs. For selectable examples, the generator associates a point with an observed match only when exactly one regenerated paired match lies within 2 pixels of both projections. These associations are recovered evidence, not preserved original IDs. Ambiguous examples are omitted. The visible residual is recomputed for this association and need not reproduce every original statistic.

The ray diagram is a top-down X–Z projection of sampled original coordinates, with Y omitted; it cannot show full 3D parallax. The residual panel magnifies actual pixel differences. Camera centers and points use arbitrary baseline units, not meters. Scene 01 and Scene 02 have independent coordinate frames.

The browser draws precomputed data; it does not run reconstruction. Small residuals and broad image coverage do not prove correct depths. The coverage rule cannot distinguish different depths merely by checking top and bottom halves.

AP measurements and what comes next

Scene 01: 2.96 s elapsed, 1.16 s Python CPU. Scene 02: 3.06 s elapsed, 1.20 s Python CPU. Both are single Pi runs; Python CPU excludes FFmpeg. Peak memory, energy, temperature and live latency were not measured. These small differences do not establish a performance change.

Status (16 Nov 2026): this two-view experiment is complete. A third-view consistency check was planned and then deferred, because the spatial-awareness plan keeps the head on a fixed base, which turns but does not translate. See the decision record. The reconstruction code remains a measured offline workload.
