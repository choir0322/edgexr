Connect this teaching example to the actual MobileNet model

The real model begins with a standard 3×3 convolution: 300×300×3 → 150×150×32. Its next depthwise layer has 32 separate 3×3 kernels; pointwise uses 64 filters, each 1×1×32, producing 150×150×64. These extra channels are learned feature maps, not extra camera colors.

The actual MobileNet backbone disables convolution biases and follows its convolutions with BatchNorm/Scale and ReLU. Biases are enabled here only to teach the general convolution operation. This comparison omits those operations to isolate convolution arithmetic. Depthwise uses one kernel per input channel (depth multiplier 1). Some libraries use “kernel” to mean the whole filter tensor; this page calls its 2D slices kernels.

54 versus 33 counts stored kernel weights; adding 2 versus 5 biases gives 56 versus 38 teaching parameters. Across four output positions the weight calculations require 216 versus 132 multiply-accumulate operations, plus 8 versus 20 bias additions. Fewer operations do not guarantee the same speedup on the Pi.

A proposal is predicted from this image. Region-of-interest (RoI) pooling prepares features for the second stage.

Faster R-CNN also uses reference anchors in its RPN. The distinction is its proposal-and-RoI stage, not whether anchors exist. Both can execute within one network; “two-stage” does not mean two camera images or a full backbone rerun for each crop.

YOUR PI 4 / MEASURED

A practical starting point for the shared CPU.

MobileNet reduces the cost of feature extraction with depthwise separable convolutions. SSD avoids a separate region proposal / RoI detection stage. This makes the combination a reasonable candidate for a Pi that also decodes video, estimates motion and reads the IMU.

Measured with 720p MJPEG/30 fps, 300×300 MobileNet-SSD input, CPU/OpenCV two threads, basic tracking and IMU on Pi 4. Five-minute static run after two-minute warm-up; passive heatsink, no fan. Temperature rose during the run. See conditions and results. The 2 Hz target is our scheduler choice, not the network’s maximum speed.

We have not measured Faster R-CNN or an alternative detector on this setup. Architecture alone cannot establish a speed or accuracy winner. Small objects, missed detections and grayscale scenes still need evaluation; model comparisons remain planned.
